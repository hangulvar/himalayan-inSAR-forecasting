### Meso Scale Landslide Susceptibility Mapping: Batote (Chakwa Nala) to Ganpat Bridge (Doda)

#### Executive Summary

This briefing document details the findings of a meso-scale (1:10,000) landslide susceptibility mapping project conducted by the Geological Survey of India (GSI) during the 2024-25 field season. The study focuses on a critical 50-line-kilometer stretch of National Highway 244 (NH-244) between Batote and Ganpat Bridge, traversing the Ramban and Doda districts of Jammu and Kashmir.The investigation utilized a heuristic-cum-deterministic model to categorize the terrain into susceptibility classes. Key findings indicate that nearly 30% of the study area falls into a "High Susceptibility" category, with instability primarily driven by complex geological structures, moisture-sensitive overburden, and unscientific slope cutting. The study identified six critical sectors requiring priority stabilization: Barthal-Buddi Mari, west of Baggar, SSW of Turungla, Marsu-Raggi Nala, Malhori section, and the Khaleni-Raggi corridor. With a model validation Success-Rate Curve (AUC) of 0.84, the resulting susceptibility maps provide a scientifically robust framework for infrastructure planning and disaster mitigation along this strategically vital transport corridor.

#### 1\. Project Context and Strategic Significance

The National Highway 244 (NH-244) serves as a vital artery connecting the mountainous interiors of Jammu and Kashmir with the rest of India. Its significance is multifaceted:

* **Strategic & Defense:**  Proximity to international borders and use for defense logistics.  
* **Economic & Energy:**  Provides essential access to major hydroelectric projects, including Dul Hasti, Pakal Dul, and Karthei.  
* **Tourism:**  Connects key destinations like Sinthan Pass and supports religious tourism.  
* **Connectivity:**  Serves as a primary route for local communities in the Doda, Kishtwar, and Anantnag districts.Frequent disruptions caused by landslides, particularly during monsoons, necessitate this high-resolution mapping to support the long-term goal of reducing socio-economic impacts and ensuring safer infrastructure development.

#### 2\. Geological and Structural Framework

The study area is situated within the Lesser Himalayan sequence and is characterized by a complex tectonic environment involving three prominent zones: the Nappe Zone, the Parautochthonous Zone, and the Foreland Zone.

##### 2.1 Lithological Units

The region comprises a diverse assemblage of Proterozoic and Neoproterozoic formations:| Formation | Lithology Characteristics || \------ | \------ || **Salkhala** | Oldest unit (occupies \~80% of some areas); carbonaceous phyllite, graphitic schist, quartzite, limestone, and basic intrusives. || **Ramban** | Slate, quartzite, arenite, and carbonaceous phyllite. || **Sincha** | Siliceous dolomite, argillite, and quartzite. || **Baila** | Platy limestone and carbonaceous argillite. || **Murree Group** | Red to purple shale, sandstone, and siltstone (Foreland Zone). || **Quaternary** | Alluvium, moraine, and scree deposits. |

##### 2.2 Structural Instability

The region is intersected by major structural boundaries, including the  **Panjal Thrust (PT)**  and the  **Digdole Thrust (DT)** . Analysis of 348 joint planes revealed specific failure patterns:

* **Planar and Wedge Failures:**  Predominantly controlled by J2 and J3 joint sets, particularly in the Raggi-Marsu-Baggar sector.  
* **Toppling Failures:**  Primarily controlled by J1 and J4 joint sets.

#### 3\. Landslide Inventory and Failure Domains

Field investigations verified 35 distinct slope instability incidences, which were documented using a 42-point geo-parametric format.

##### 3.1 Breakdown of Instability Types

* **Rock Slides:**  11 instances  
* **Rock Falls:**  10 instances  
* **Debris Slides:**  9 instances  
* **Creep Zones:**  2 instances  
* **Subsidence Zones:**  2 instances  
* **Slumps:**  1 instance

##### 3.2 Failure Domains

The study calculated Total Estimated Susceptibility Values (TESV) across four primary domains:

1. **Cut-slope domain**  
2. **Debris slide domain**  
3. **Rock slide domain**  
4. **Rock-fall domain**

#### 4\. Geotechnical Evaluation

Comprehensive testing of 20 soil samples and 10 rock samples at GSI-NR laboratories in Lucknow provided critical data on material strength and stability.

##### 4.1 Rock Mass Properties

* **Unconfined Compressive Strength (UCS):**  Ranges from 34 to 748 kgf/cm², indicating highly variable rock competence.  
* **Slope Mass Rating (SMR):**  107 outcrops were classified as:  
* **Fair (Class III):**  41%  
* **Poor (Class IV):**  41%  
* **Good (Class II):**  23%  
* Zones of "Poor" rating coincide largely with weathered quartzite, schist, and carbonaceous lithologies.

##### 4.2 Overburden and Soil Characteristics

The overburden, composed of colluvium, scree, and River Borne Material (RBM), varies in thickness from 0.5 meters to over 20 meters.

* **Grain-Size Analysis:**  75% of samples contained more than 75% fines (\<0.075 mm), indicating silty, low-permeability material.  
* **Moisture Sensitivity:**  Low to intermediate plasticity (Atterberg limits) suggests rapid strength loss during saturation.  
* **Shear Strength:**  While cohesion (mean 18.5 kg/cm²) and friction angles (36.4°–39.1°) indicate good dry strength, there is a significant reduction when wet, leading to shallow translational or debris slides.

#### 5\. Landslide Susceptibility Mapping (LSM) Results

The integration of thematic geofactor maps (slope angle, aspect, relative relief, drainage, SPI, and LULC) with field data resulted in a comprehensive susceptibility classification.

##### 5.1 Susceptibility Classification

Susceptibility Class,Area Percentage  
Low,34.78%  
Moderate,35.25%  
High,29.97%

##### 5.2 Model Validation

The model was validated using a Success-Rate Curve, achieving an  **Area Under the Curve (AUC) of \~0.84** . This indicates a high level of predictive accuracy and strong spatial agreement between documented landslides and zones identified as highly susceptible.

#### 6\. Critical Sectors and Management Recommendations

The mapping identified six priority sectors that exhibit high susceptibility and require immediate stabilization or specialized management:

1. **Barthal-Buddi Mari**  
2. **West of Baggar**  
3. **SSW of Turungla**  
4. **Marsu-Raggi Nala**  
5. **Malhori Section**  
6. **Khaleni-Raggi Corridor**  (instability often linked to recent excavations and unscientific slope cutting).

##### 6.1 General Recommendations

* **Mitigation Planning:**  Use the 1:10,000 scale maps to design site-specific engineering interventions.  
* **Infrastructure Design:**  Future highway projects and maintenance should account for the moisture sensitivity of silty overburden.  
* **Monitoring:**  Continuous monitoring of identified creep and subsidence zones.  
* **Stakeholder Integration:**  The Landslide Management Plan is designed for direct use by state administration and infrastructure developers to foster community preparedness and sustainable development.

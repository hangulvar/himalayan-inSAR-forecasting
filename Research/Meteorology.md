To learn meteorology, you'll need to understand the underlying principles of atmospheric science before delving into forecasting techniques. Wallace & Hobbs's "Atmospheric Science" is a respected textbook often used in university programs for its comprehensive approach to these fundamental principles. 

However, if you're looking for the best free resources, here are some options that offer different approaches:

1. National Weather Service (NWS) Jetstream

- **Content:** This platform provides a detailed breakdown of meteorology basics, similar to an introductory textbook, including topics like weather maps, atmospheric dynamics, and severe weather phenomena.
- **Target Audience:** Ideal for those new to meteorology who want a clear and accessible introduction. 

2. MetEd COMET Modules

- **Content:** MetEd, run by the University Corporation for Atmospheric Research (UCAR), offers interactive modules covering a wide array of meteorology topics.
- **Key Feature:** Requires a free account to access, but provides in-depth, university-level material suitable for dedicated learners. 

3. Harvard University's "Backyard Meteorology: The Science of Weather" (via edX)

- **Content:** This free online course focuses on observational skills and understanding weather forecasting without advanced tools.
- **Target Audience:** Perfect for those wanting a hands-on approach to learning about weather phenomena and building foundational observation skills. 

4. Open University's "Watching the weather" (via OpenLearn)

- **Content:** This free course teaches about different methods of meteorological observations, from surface-level data to satellite and aerial observations.
- **Key Feature:** Offers a free certificate upon completion, providing a verifiable record of your learning.  

5. Jeff Haby's Hints

- **Content:** This resource simplifies complex meteorological concepts into easy-to-understand language through articles covering various weather topics.
- **Key Feature:** Excellent for those who appreciate clear and straightforward explanations and want to quickly grasp meteorological concepts. 

6. The Forecaster's Handbook Series by Tim Vasquez

- **Content:** While not entirely free, two recommended books for beginners are the "Weather Analysis and Forecasting Handbook" and the "Weather Map Handbook".
- **Key Feature:** Provides comprehensive crash courses in the basics of meteorology for those who prefer learning from books. 

7. Storm Prediction Center Video Lecture Series

- **Content:** Offers video lectures from experts in the field, including those responsible for severe weather forecasting.
- **Target Audience:** Suitable for those interested in a deeper dive into meteorological concepts, similar to an upper-level college course. 

8. MIT OpenCourseWare (OCW)

- **Content:** While a specific "Introduction to Weather Forecasting" (Course 12.310) was mentioned, the availability of that specific course might require checking the current MIT OCW catalog. However, MIT OCW offers various courses in earth, atmospheric, and planetary sciences, including courses like "Tropical Meteorology" (12.811), "Atmospheric Physics and Chemistry" (10.571J), and "Dynamics of the Atmosphere" (12.810).
- **Key Feature:** Provides a free lecture on meteorology as part of its Private Pilot Ground School course, covering weather theory, patterns, and aviation hazards. 

**Choosing the best option depends on your learning style:**

- **Visual learners:** May prefer the NWS Jetstream or videos from the Storm Prediction Center.
- **Hands-on learners:** Might appreciate the interactive modules from MetEd COMET or Harvard's Backyard Meteorology course.
- **Those who prefer structured learning with verification:** Might find the Open University course with a free certificate appealing. 

Remember to explore these resources and find the one that resonates most with your learning preferences and goals.
